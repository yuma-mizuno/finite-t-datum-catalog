Objects and equivalence

Fix a rank n, meaning the number of species. A datum consists of polynomial matrices A₊, A₋ and a positive integer diagonal matrix D = diag(d₁,…,dₙ), with

N₀(z) = diag(1 + zr₁, …, 1 + zrₙ),   A±(z) = N₀(z) − N±(z),   rᵢ ∈ ℤ>0.

The coefficients of N± are nonnegative integers. In entry (i,j), every support exponent satisfies 0 < p < rᵢ. The two signs have disjoint support in each entry. In addition, D−1N±D has integral coefficients, and the weighted symplectic identity is required:

A₊(z)D A₋(z−1)T = A₋(z)D A₊(z−1)T.

These are the diagonal-leading specialization of Mizuno’s general T-datum definition. Finite type is periodicity of the associated universal T- and Y-systems; the equivalences in Theorem 5.4 relate this to tropical periodicity. A datum is indecomposable when the two polynomial matrices cannot be made simultaneously block diagonal with two nonempty blocks.

The catalogue records primitive D, with gcd(d₁,…,dₙ) = 1. If scalar multiples of D are retained as distinct triples, every representative additionally carries the free parameter D ↦ mD, m ∈ ℤ>0; the polynomial matrices and their dynamics are unchanged. Decomposable triples are direct sums of indecomposable blocks, allowing independent admissible time scales, sign choices and positive scalar factors on their block symmetrizers.

Within a polynomial lift family, the transformations are

A′±(z) = E(z) A±(zλ) E(z)−1,
E(z) = diag(zs₁,…,zsₙ₋₁,1),   λ ∈ ℚ>0,   sᵢ ∈ ℚ.

Only parameters giving integral delays and support exponents with the strict row bounds are admissible. Simultaneous species permutations permute D; sign exchange preserves it. The slice comparison retains the vertex symmetrizer during cyclic rotations and interchanges of commuting mutations. Langlands duality, (A±,D) ↦ (D−1A±D, primitive D−1), is not imposed as an additional equivalence. General nonidentity leading permutations are outside this catalogue’s scope.

Why the symmetrizer search is finite

Put P = N₊(1), M = N₋(1). The positivity theorem, Mizuno, Theorem 5.5, gives a common positive row vector v with v(2I−P) > 0 and v(2I−M) > 0 for every finite-type datum.

Strong connectivity of the union support

Suppose the union directed support were reducible. Order its strongly connected components so that A± are block upper triangular. The rational matrix K(z) = A₊(z)−1A₋(z) satisfies KD = DK(z−1)T. Its upper triangular and reciprocal lower triangular forms force K to be block diagonal.

Since A±(0) = I, every diagonal block of K has constant term I. The equation A₋ = A₊K then forces the lowest nonzero coefficient of any off-block N₋ entry to agree with that of N₊ in the same position. This contradicts disjoint support. Thus all off-block entries vanish. Indecomposability therefore forces strong connectivity.

A bound for coefficients and primitive weights

For a directed edge i → j, let wᵢⱼ = max(Pᵢⱼ,Mᵢⱼ). Positivity gives wᵢⱼvᵢ < 2vⱼ. Every edge lies on a simple directed cycle of length at most n. Multiplying these inequalities around the cycle yields a product of positive integers less than 2n. Consequently every off-diagonal coefficient sum is at most B = 2n−1. Diagonal sums are 0 or 1.

The dual matrices P∨ = D−1PD and M∨ = D−1MD are integral and have the same support. The positive row vector vD satisfies the same inequalities for them. Their coefficient sums therefore obey the same bound. In particular, for every edge of original weight c,

q = c dⱼ/dᵢ ∈ {1,…,B}.

Choose a directed spanning tree rooted at one vertex. Each tree edge gives a ratio dⱼ/dᵢ = q/c. Multiply all tree denominators to obtain an integral vector proportional to D. Every entry is a product of at most n−1 factors, each at most B. Primitive normalization can only decrease the entries. Thus

1 ≤ dᵢ ≤ (2n−1)n−1.

For n = 6 this is 635 = 992,436,543. The algorithm does not scan this box. It solves the homogeneous linear equations in D and branches only on bounded integral edge ratios. The bound proves that no unlisted large primitive symmetrizer is being silently excluded.

The weighted parity condition

The diagonal Laurent polynomial (A₊(z)D A₋(z−1)T)ᵢᵢ/dᵢ has integral coefficients, by dual integrality. It is reciprocal. Its constant coefficient is exactly 2: all possible N₊N₋ constant contributions vanish by disjoint support, and the strict support bounds exclude cross terms with N₀. Nonzero exponents consequently occur in equal opposite pairs. Evaluation at z = 1 gives

((2I−P)D(2I−M)T)ᵢᵢ ∈ 2dᵢ ℤ.

This is stronger than demanding merely an even weighted diagonal entry, and is invariant under a common scalar change of D.

Exact constant enumeration

A signed weight partition from the diagonal Laurent identity

Write cᵢₖ = PᵢₖMᵢₖ. In the cross-product Hᵢ(z) = ∑ₖdₖN₊,ᵢₖ(z)N₋,ᵢₖ(z⁻¹), no exponent is zero, by disjoint support. The diagonal symplectic identity says that Hᵢ, together with its leading/diagonal-N terms, is reciprocal. Every leading/diagonal-N contribution has one strictly positive and one strictly negative exponent with equal coefficient, because 0 < p < rᵢ. Its total mass on the two sides is therefore equal. The same must hold for Hᵢ.

Split each positive integer coefficient into unit copies. If tₖ is the number of copies at positive exponents minus the number at negative exponents in the kth product, necessarily

∑ₖ tₖdₖ = 0,   tₖ ∈ {−cᵢₖ, −cᵢₖ+2, …, cᵢₖ}.

This holds for every row, including rows with diagonal N terms. In particular, a single overlapping column with odd cᵢₖ is impossible. Two overlapping monomials force their column weights to be equal. The enumerator branches over these necessary linear equations when there are at most 512 combinations; when there are more, it skips this optional pruning rule. The number 512 is not a bound on data or delays. If every cᵢₖ is even, the zero equation is available and no branch is needed. The independent partition controls compare the complete rank-four and rank-five enumerations with separately filtered reference sets and check the signed masses directly on every previously verified polynomial representative.

Once D is fixed, dual integrality strengthens the same test: put gᵢₖ = dᵢ/gcd(dᵢ,dₖ), replace cᵢₖ by (Pᵢₖ/gᵢₖ)(Mᵢₖ/gᵢₖ), and replace its weight dₖ by dₖgᵢₖ². These are exactly the number and weight of the integral coefficient-atom products. The polynomial replay applies this finite necessary partition test when it has at most 4096 choices, skipping it otherwise. A failure is an analytic exclusion from the diagonal identity, independently recomputed during replay.

Order v₁ ≥ ··· ≥ vₙ. In either sign, the part of a column above the diagonal is zero, or consists of a single 1, the latter forcing the diagonal entry to be zero. There are (n+1)! upper triangles per sign. All unordered sign pairs are processed. Directed path bounds and integral column inequalities reduce the bounded choices for lower rows.

For fixed completed rows, the constant weighted symplectic identity is a homogeneous linear system in d₁,…,dₙ. Integer row reduction either gives a unique primitive positive diagonal or leaves ratios to choose. For an existing edge, its integral dual coefficient is in {1,…,B}. Adding an independent ratio equation lowers the kernel dimension. At complete constants, strong connectivity supplies enough edge ratios to finish this finite enumeration.

Every known directed cycle has equal products of original and dual coefficients. The possible dual coefficient on an edge therefore divides each known cycle product through that edge. Proportional coordinates on the current kernel also allow early integrality and weighted parity tests. These tests discard only impossible diagonals.

Schur complements and simultaneous positivity

For a nonnegative matrix C, write the enlarged leading block of 2I−C as [[H,−u],[−w,a]], where H is already a nonsingular M-matrix. Its Schur complement is a−wH−1u. Because H−1, u and w are nonnegative, positivity of this scalar is equivalent to the enlarged block being a nonsingular M-matrix. The block inverse formula proves its inverse is nonnegative, and all principal minors are positive.

After multiplication by det H, this is an exact affine budget for the new nonnegative row entries. The row enumerator maintains these budgets for both signs, or solves one sign’s row by an exact adjugate and checks the resulting linear inequalities. This avoids repeatedly recomputing equivalent principal minors. The independent final verifier still checks every principal minor of every retained constant candidate.

Positive principal minors for the two signs separately do not establish a common positive vector. Earlier enumerators test exact extreme rays of the ordered simultaneous-positive cone. The final enumerator uses the following stronger row budgets, which also give an elementary equivalent finite test.

Mixed-column lemma. Let A and B be Z-matrices (nonpositive off-diagonal entries). A common row vector v > 0 with vA > 0 and vB > 0 exists if and only if every matrix formed by choosing each column independently from A or B is a nonsingular M-matrix.

Necessity follows by applying the common vector to each chosen column. For sufficiency, every columnwise convex combination C has positive principal minors: each such determinant is a convex combination, by multilinearity, of the corresponding positive minors of the pure column choices. Therefore C is a nonsingular M-matrix and C⁻¹ is nonnegative. If the required strict vector did not exist, Gordan’s alternative applied to the columns of [I,A,B] would give x,u,w ≥ 0, not all zero, with x + Au + Bw = 0. Put t = u+w. It is nonzero, and choosing column mixture weights uᵢ/tᵢ gives Ct = −x ≤ 0. Multiplication by C⁻¹ yields t ≤ 0, a contradiction.

The implementation maintains the exact Schur budget of every mixed-column leading block. Equal prefix columns are compressed: their independent new-row choices contribute the maximum of the two row entries. This enforces all the original budgets exactly. The lemma then supplies common positivity at the final row. The independent verifier still constructs a common positive rational vector using a separate linear-arithmetic solver for every retained constant triple.

Principal subdata and inherited symmetrizers

Let S be a proper set of species. Suppose every exterior column k has either N₊[S,k] = 0 or N₋[S,k] = 0. Every exterior-column term in the symplectic identity on rows S then vanishes, including its factor dₖ. Thus the principal matrices on S, with the restriction of D, form a T-datum. Dual integrality is preserved.

Deleting exterior rows removes nonpositive contributions from the column products vA±, so simultaneous positivity restricts as well. Split the principal datum into weakly connected components and normalize each restricted D. Each must occur in the completed lower-rank classification under simultaneous positivity. This argument uses that lower-rank polynomial classification; it does not assume that arbitrary principal restriction preserves periodicity.

The enumerator checks eligible completed subsets. It also generates possible new rows directly from the classified lower-rank constant blocks and their direct sums. A table forgetting D supplies a necessary test. A table retaining D supplies the further weight-ratio equations whenever the lower constant pair has a unique primitive symmetrizer. Uniqueness is checked for every labelled table key; an ambiguous table causes the implementation to stop. Inherited equations and explicitly chosen ratio branches are carried to subsequent rows.

Canonicalization retains D together with P and M, exhausts species permutations and sign exchange, and never merges triples by polynomial matrices alone. The saved upper tasks may use different proved pruning rules; their union is a complete set of necessary candidates for polynomial lifts, rather than necessarily the unpruned set of constant solutions. Splitting a task resets its successful-positivity cache and can remove redundant appearances in particular tasks, while retaining the complete canonical union.

The brute-force and ratio controls, row-completion controls, subset controls, task-partition controls and mixed-positivity controls record comparisons with independent reference computations. Early coefficient-sign tests discard a partially specified homogeneous equation only when every possible completion has coefficients of one sign and a nonzero coefficient. All arithmetic is integral or rational. Compiled signed-overflow checks stop on overflow instead of accepting a wrapped result.

All polynomial lifts, without a delay cutoff

For entry (i,j), dual integrality means every coefficient is a multiple of gᵢⱼ = dᵢ/gcd(dᵢ,dⱼ). A coefficient sum c is therefore represented by c/gᵢⱼ ordered exponent variables, each carrying coefficient gᵢⱼ. Coincident atoms are allowed. Opposite-sign exponents in the same entry are required to be distinct.

The delays and atom exponents are unbounded positive integer variables with 0 < p < rᵢ. Expand each weighted Laurent identity into two multisets of integral linear forms in these variables. Equality of the multisets is an exact finite linear-arithmetic encoding of the polynomial identity. One may equivalently use positive rational variables with homogeneous normalization and clear denominators; no maximum delay is introduced.

Every feasible witness determines a rational matching space. Its equations are checked symbolically, and a further query asks for a valid lift outside the union of recorded spaces. An UNSAT answer proves coverage. In every completed family here the space has dimension n and is spanned by its representative together with the n−1 relative species shifts. The exact rank computation identifies the entire family, including every admissible rational scaling and shift.

Principal lower-rank spaces give necessary linear equations in the larger lift variables. Existing identity-symmetrizer obstructions are applied only to D = I. Analytic exclusions record their lemma and lower-rank dependency; a saved trivial UNSAT query in such a case is only a record of the exclusion, and the verifier separately recomputes its mathematical condition. Solver timeouts remain unresolved and cannot support a completeness claim.

For every surviving representative, formula (3.7) of Mizuno’s paper reconstructs the skew-symmetrizable exchange matrix from original and dual coefficients. Vertex (i,p) has right symmetrizer dᵢ. Exact principal-coefficient mutation verifies loop closure, negative permutation C-matrices in both directions, and a labelled tropical seed return. Theorem 5.4 then supplies universal periodicity. Slice distinctness compares weighted terminal cycle lengths and, within collision groups, complete cyclic-loop orbits under rotations, commuting mutations and both signs.

Matrix ratios, exponents and family notes

The reader records both ordered rational products K = A₊(1)−1A₋(1) and K−1 = A₋(1)−1A₊(1). They are checked by exact multiplication. For nonidentity D the fixed-point matrix in the thesis is the dual matrix:

K∨ = D−1KD,   log f = K∨ log(1−f),   0 < fᵢ < 1.

The determinant uses the original polynomial matrices:

τ̄(z) = det(A₊(z) diag(1−f) + A₋(z) diag(f)),
τ(z) = τ̄(z1/t),   N = Ω/t.

Here t is the number of connected components of the full representative and Ω its recorded period. The Cartan-like centering shifts are checked. The formula and conventions are Mizuno’s thesis, Theorem 3.2.4, equation (3.4.1), Definition 3.4.4 and Theorem 3.4.5. KD is checked symmetric positive definite, and the positive fixed point is enclosed by a Krawczyk inclusion.

Interval Newton sums and Fourier inversion on the exact period grid isolate each root multiplicity in a single nonnegative integer. The periodicity theorem supplies integrality; numerical rounding alone does not. A separate interval computation of the logarithmic mutation Jacobian, using thesis equation (3.4.2), recovers the same spectrum. The unit-disk plot places the root exp(2πim/N) at exponent m, labels repeated roots by their algebraic multiplicity, and has total multiplicity equal to the connected-slice dimension.

Named-family notes are exact polynomial comparisons with finite Cartan and tadpole tensor constructions, retaining D. Degree-two data are also checked against the commuting-Cartan construction of thesis Proposition 2.3.4. A record with no match is labelled “No named family identified”; this is not a novelty claim, and does not exclude an untested folding description.

Certified polynomial folds of SG and RSG records

For a partition of an identity-symmetrizer representative, let E be its species-to-block incidence matrix and S = EᵀE the diagonal matrix of block sizes. Equal delays within a block, and row and column equitability of both polynomial matrices, give A±E = EQ± and A±ᵀE = ES⁻¹Q±ᵀS. Restricting the symplectic identity to this common invariant subspace proves Q₊S⁻¹Q₋† = Q₋S⁻¹Q₊†. Thus the quotient has right symmetrizer proportional to S⁻¹. Its dual matrices SQ±S⁻¹ are integral column quotients.

The computation checks equitability after the parent’s recorded Cartan-like centering. It separately checks the strict support bounds and opposite-sign disjointness, which need not survive an arbitrary quotient. It then matches the quotient, or its Langlands dual, exactly to a classified representative. A second replay verifies the incidence-matrix equations and the complete source-to-record polynomial transformation. Notes called “Fold of …” record this computed structural relation, with a parent link and partition; they do not claim that the quotient’s displayed name was previously published. The parent library contains the named identity records through rank six, so this does not exhaust all possible unfoldings.

Quiver notes classify the valued exchange matrix separately from its mutation loop. Finite Dynkin and standard mutation-finite types carry integer mutation paths and valued graph comparisons. For a connected matrix on at least three vertices, an edge with |BᵢⱼBⱼᵢ| > 4 proves mutation-infinite type by Felikson–Shapiro–Tumarkin, Theorem 2.8. Other finite classes are recorded by a complete mutation orbit, either labelled or up to weight-preserving relabelling. In the latter case every transition carries an explicit vertex permutation, and an independent replay checks the integer matrix equality and reachability of every stored state. The control computation recovers the same quotients from the previously complete small labelled orbits. Equal canonical orbit identifiers link occurrences across ranks. The quiver class alone does not determine the T-datum or its exponents.

Reproduction and proof obligations

The final completion audit checks all rank counts against the preserved identity catalogue and verifies the archived query hashes. The source manifest and source archive preserve the current verification code and every historical constant-enumerator source used. The control archive includes the independently compared reference computations.

Each rank directory contains constant tasks, lift feasibility, matching spaces, exact exclusion or coverage replays, weighted slice signatures, spectral certificates. Archives preserve the individual tasks and SMT queries. Source hashes and Git versions identify the implementations. Unresolved solver calls are retried or resolved explicitly before a completeness claim.
